Select text. Press play.

Harness Discussion reads the text you select in any Windows or Mac app, with the voice from your Cursor or Codex sign-in. No API key. Local voices read when neither can.

What the bar does

Drag or double-click to select text in a browser, Notepad, VS Code, and most other apps. The bar shows up next to the selection and never takes focus from the app you're in.

The sign-in you already have

Reads with Cursor's voices through the Cursor desktop sign-in, or with the Codex realtime voice from your ChatGPT sign-in. No API key. Codex uses your plan's Codex access and counts toward its limits.

Always has a voice

If Cursor and Codex can't speak, Kokoro reads on your computer after a one-time 120 MB download. It covers English, Spanish, and French. Then Edge neural voices and the offline Windows voices. On a Mac, the built-in macOS voice.

Summarize

✦ next to Play summarizes the selection on your computer with Qwen3-4B, then reads the summary. The first use asks to download the model once (2.5 GB, checked by SHA-256).

Pause, skip, speed

⏸ pauses and ▶ continues from the same spot. ⏮ and ⏭ move by a sentence, and ■ stops. Pick 0.5× to 2× from the bar's speed menu. Your choice is saved.

A shortcut, too

Ctrl+Alt+R on Windows, or ⌥⌘R on a Mac, reads the selection at once. Press it again to stop. Change it in Settings → Reading.

Six languages

Hebrew, English, Arabic, Russian, Spanish, and French. In Auto, Cursor and Codex infer the language, and Edge and Windows tell Hebrew, Arabic, and Russian apart by script. Kokoro reads English, Spanish, and French. Or pick one from More (…) → Language.

Optional automatic updates

On Windows, installs new releases when the app is idle, after checking the installer's SHA-256 digest. Off by default. On a Mac, Settings shows new versions and installs one when you click Download.

Speech-to-text server

An optional local, OpenAI-compatible endpoint lets apps such as OpenWhispr transcribe with the same Codex sign-in. Details.

Rewrite and dictate (Mac only)

On a Mac, ✎ or ⌥⌘E rewrites text you can edit and pastes it back, and holding ⌥⌘D types what you say. Off by default. These aren't available on Windows.

Where your text goes

Each voice source sends reading text to a different place. For local read-aloud, enable only Kokoro and Windows or macOS in Settings → Voices.

Mac Writing and Dictation have separate source settings. For local rewrites, disable Cursor and Codex in Settings → Writing and use Local. Dictation sends microphone audio to Cursor or Codex. Turn off both sources in Settings → Dictation to disable transcription through those services.

Get started

  1. Install

    Run the installer. It installs for your user only and needs no admin rights. On a Mac, drag the app to Applications.

    The app isn't code-signed. On Windows, SmartScreen may warn you: choose More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, choose Open Anyway in Privacy & Security, then grant Accessibility access when the guide asks. You can read and build the source yourself.

    Coming from Codex Speak or Select to TTS? It's the same app under a new name. The installer replaces it and keeps your settings.

  2. Sign in

    Sign in to the Cursor desktop app, or install the Codex CLI and run codex login once. Without either, the app uses Kokoro if you download it, then the Edge and Windows voices, or the macOS voice on a Mac. The first time it starts, the app opens Settings so you can check the voice sources.

  3. Select and play

    Select text with the mouse, or on a Mac with ⇧+arrows or ⌘A, and press ▶ on the bar. ⏸ pauses, ⏮ and ⏭ move by a sentence, and ■ stops.

  4. Tune it

    Choose Settings… from the bar's More (…) menu, or click the tray or menu bar icon. Voices sets the source order and speed. Captions, Reading, Summarize, and General hold the rest, including start at sign-in and updates.

Before you install

The Codex voice is experimental

Codex's realtime API is marked experimental, and about 1 in 12 Codex reads in testing ended early or stayed silent. Silence moves the read to the next source on its own.

Apps that hide their selection

On Windows, the app reads only text that apps expose and never copies your selection, to protect your clipboard. Apps that hide their selection can't be read there. On a Mac, the app can borrow ⌘C for those apps and then puts your clipboard back. You can turn this off.

Keyboard selections on Mac only

Drag or double-click shows the bar. On a Mac, so do ⇧+arrows and ⌘A. On Windows, keyboard selections don't.

Uninstall

On Windows, use Settings > Apps. This removes the app, its sign-in task, and the Kokoro model. Your preferences stay in %APPDATA%\harness-discussion.
